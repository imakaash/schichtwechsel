# Data card: synthetic HR data

The `hris` schema holds HR data for a **fictional** drugstore chain. No row describes a real person or a real employer. It exists so the models and the analyst agent can be built and shown without touching personal data.

Generated by `sw hris generate` (`src/schichtwechsel/hris/generate.py`). The same seed and the same inputs give the same data. Each run records its seed, real inputs and true coefficients in `hris.generation`.

## What is real and what is not

| Input | Source |
|---|---|
| Competitor hiring pressure per Bundesland, store roles | Real: `mart.hiring_pressure`, from BA job postings |
| Market hourly wage for store staff | Real: median stated pay in drugstore competitor postings (15.00 €, n = 51 on 2026-10-25) |
| Market apprentice pay | Real but thin: median stated monthly pay (1,180 €, n = 7) |
| Stores per Bundesland | Proportional to population (Destatis 2024) |
| Regional wage spread around the market wage | Assumption, ±5% (`REGION_WAGE`) |
| Everything else: people, tenure, hours, exits, openings | Simulated |

The pressure index is a single snapshot, applied as a fixed regional level. It varies across regions, not over time. Once daily snapshots have built up a history, the generator can take a time series instead.

Months run from 2023-01 to 2026-09.

## How it is generated

**Stores.** 300, spread over the Bundesländer in proportion to population. Sizes S, M and L (8, 14 and 22 planned staff, shares 35/45/20%). Each store has a pay offset around the regional wage (sd 0.35 €/h) and is urban or not.

**Staffing.** Each store has a manager, a deputy, one to three apprentices, and sales associates split 25% full time, 45% part time and 30% minijob. The starting workforce has realistic tenure (exponential, mean 48 months; 18 for minijobs).

**People.** Age is drawn by contract type: apprentices 16 to 22, minijobbers a mix of students, mid-career workers and retirees, everyone else around 38. **No gender or name is generated.** Wages follow the regional market wage, the store offset, a personal offset and +0.20 €/h per year of tenure, all growing 3% a year with the market.

**Exits each month:**
1. Apprentices finish after 36 months; 55% stay on as full-time sales associates.
2. Fixed-term contracts (40% of new hires, 12 or 24 months) end; 80% convert to permanent.
3. Retirement at 66.
4. Voluntary exit with a monthly probability from the drivers below.
5. Involuntary exit, 0.3% a month.

Every exit except an apprentice's opens a requisition. The new hire starts the month after it is filled, and until then the open slot raises the store's vacancy rate. A new apprentice cohort starts every August.

## True drivers of voluntary exit

Monthly log-odds, added to a base of -4.7 and a per-person random effect (sd 0.5) that no model can see:

| Driver | Effect |
|---|---|
| Minijob / part time / apprentice (vs. full time) | +1.0 / +0.25 / -1.0 |
| Tenure under 6 months / 6 to 24 / over 60 | +0.9 / +0.35 / -0.45 |
| Age under 25 / 55 and over | +0.45 / -0.3 |
| Pay ratio (own wage / market wage - 1) | -4.0 per unit; 10% under market is +0.4 |
| Store manager changed in the last 6 months | +0.6 |
| Store vacancy rate | +2.5 per unit |
| Competitor pressure, log(index / 100) | +0.8 per unit; index 140 is +0.27, index 50 is -0.55 |
| Fixed-term contract ends within 2 months | +0.5 |
| January / August and September | +0.3 / +0.2 |

The result is about 25% voluntary turnover a year: about 53% for minijobs, 18% for part time and 14% for full time.

## True drivers of time to fill and applicants

Log days to fill: log(32) + 0.6 × log(pressure / 100) + role effect (minijob -0.15, deputy +0.35, manager +0.6) + 0.2 if opened in November or December - 0.1 if urban - 3.0 × store pay level, plus noise (sd 0.45). Applicants are Poisson around 14, fewer under high pressure (-0.7 × log pressure), more with better pay, and fewer for manager and deputy roles. Medians: 29 days for sales associates, 44 for deputies, 58 for managers; 13 applicants.

## Intended use

- Building and testing the attrition and time-to-fill models, including a check that the models recover the drivers above.
- Demonstrating the analyst agent and the privacy rules (aggregates only, groups under 5 suppressed).

## Not for

- Any statement about a real employer's workforce. The chain is invented; only the competitor inputs are real.
- Estimating real effect sizes. The effects above are design choices, plausible for German retail but not measured.

## Known limits

- Headcount drifts up about 3% over the period, because retained apprentices stay on above plan.
- Age influences the simulated exits but is left out of the models' inputs, since age is a protected attribute under the AGG. Model fairness is checked by comparing calibration across age bands.
- No sick leave, overtime, performance or engagement data. Real HR systems would add these as drivers, and their absence makes the problem easier than in practice.
- Competitor pressure is static in time (see above).
