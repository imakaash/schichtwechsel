# 10. Synthetic HR data, and simple models checked against known truth

Date: 2026-10-25
Status: accepted

## Context

The job asks for forecasting and evaluation models on HR data. Real HR data is personal data and not available for a portfolio project. Public HR datasets (the IBM attrition set) are overused and have no link to the labour market.

## Decision

- Generate HR data for a fictional chain from documented drivers (`docs/data-card.md`), with two real inputs: competitor hiring pressure per Bundesland and the market wage from competitor postings. The link between internal and external data is the point of the project.
- Because the true drivers are known, every model is checked three ways: against a held-out later period, against the ceiling set by the true formula, and by comparing estimated effects with true ones.
- Main models are linear (logistic for the monthly attrition hazard, linear on log days for time to fill). Gradient boosting runs as a challenger and is reported even though it lost.
- No gender in the data. Age is generated but is never a model input; calibration by age band is reported, and showed the 55+ group over-predicted by a factor of two. Individual scores stay in `hris`; everything in `mart` is aggregated with groups under five dropped.
- The posting-volume forecast per competitor is deferred. It needs history; there is one snapshot day so far. It becomes possible after about eight weeks of daily snapshots.

## Consequences

- Both main models sit at their ceilings, and most effects are recovered within 10 to 20 percent. That shows the pipeline works, not that it would do this well on real data, where drivers are unknown and the data was not generated by a logistic formula.
- The age finding is a real result worth discussing: leaving a protected attribute out does not remove its influence.
- Writing the checks found three bugs that would otherwise have shipped: a fixed-term flag that covered a month where leaving was impossible, an exit rate that averaged over leavers only (NULL handling), and a DuckDB result left half-read.
